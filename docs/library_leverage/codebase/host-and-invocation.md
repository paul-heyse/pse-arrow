# Host and invocation

This page covers `pse-host` (the `pse`, `pse-host` and `cargo-pse-correctness` binaries), the Rust control plane for work on this workstation: environment composition, systemd placement, admission, detached invocations, force-validation, test capture and immutable artifact generations. Its architecture owners are [workspace and dependencies](../../authoritative_design/sections/workspace-and-dependencies.md) and the [agent environment guide](../../dev/agent-environment.md). Refreshed 2026-11-02 against clean source `f8b7d74d8232da58bf485e6e16f5bfde969c2dd7`; publication edits are assessment documents only. Criteria are framework/base edition 0.2 and additional-pattern revision 5. Mechanisms below are *Interface-checked* by static reading; every value claim is *Proposed*. No build, provider preparation, test or measurement was performed.

**System picture.** The host places workloads in user-systemd scopes, services and slices, and authenticates what it finds. It supports the maintainer's unbounded ordinary placement and explicit finite/scientific selection, runs detached invocations with write-once evidence, forces Arrow validation into correctness builds, captures test binaries and interprets nextest streams, and constructs immutable artifacts: its own tools, native solver providers, the patched SurrealDB server, study modules and downloaded tools. Agents and the maintainer consume it through `scripts/pse`, `scripts/pse-env` and native command families; xtask and test fixtures use it too. Source, schema, API and compiler investigations now add substantial consumers of immutable artifacts and prepared tools. Those developer-tooling opportunities have their own [primary home](developer-tooling.md).

`manager::Group` retains an opened cgroup owner; launch subscribes before dispatch, and managed execution now uses a single-service lifecycle. `invocation::publish` remains write-once with `RENAME_NOREPLACE` and directory fsync; `pse_diagnostics::SupervisedChild` consolidates child supervision; `catalog::command_tree` derives commands from clap. The wrappers exec prepared native tools. Automatic host-generation collection and the former cross-process generation-use claim protocol have been removed. Their absence is an explicit retention choice, not a newly discovered defect. Opportunities cluster in repeated artifact publication, invocation evidence management, Cargo interposition and provisioning.

Neighbouring host concerns live elsewhere: private-file and atomic-publication primitives are [CBO-003](cross-cutting-mechanisms.md#cbo-003), process identity is [CBO-004](cross-cutting-mechanisms.md#cbo-004), `/proc` parsing is [CBO-019](execution-lifecycle-and-observation.md#cbo-019), polling waits are [CBO-016](execution-lifecycle-and-observation.md#cbo-016), and the manager's existing accounting and remaining peak/OOM questions, the test and check front doors, and the nextest seam are on [developer tooling](developer-tooling.md).

**Coverage.** All six live opportunities were reconsidered. This pass read `InvocationStore::{resolve,history}`, `correctness::AdmissionContext::resolve_with`, native/server/study-module publication bodies, current generation and entry ownership, bootstrap and tool declarations, and the identified subprocess consumers; provider and correctness test declarations were inspected as dependencies, not executed. Native source/syntax inspection was used without analyzer preparation. Provider construction remains partly inherited from the 2026-10-30 reading: `verify_petsc`, `relocate_metadata`, `high_provider` and server release-import internals were located rather than traced. Earlier manager/admission reads are historical context, not a claim that the changed lifecycle was exhaustively re-audited. Host disk counts and filesystem observations below retain their original 2026-10-30 date.

## CBO-054 — Immutable artifact generations implemented once per kind

- **Operation and consumers.** Construct or fetch an artifact, record provenance, seal it, publish a selection pointer, and later reopen it without sources. Consumers are launch, fixtures, tests and `doctor`.
- **Current mechanism.** At least five parallel owners: `generation` and `generation/bootstrap` for host tools (`GenerationManifest`, `selected`, `publish`); `native` and `native/providers` (`commit_provider`, `publish_generation`, `publish_pointer`); `server` (`seal`, `immutable`, `validate`, `publish_pointer`, `publish_install`, `file_map`, `tree`); `study_module` (`publish`, receipts); `diagnostic_tools` (`verify_bundle`, `publish_provenance`). `product_reader` and `prepared_xtask` repeat a smaller `Evidence` and manifest form that reads as a copy between the two. Immutability is a recursive chmod plus a recursive re-verify on selection (`server::seal`, `server::immutable`), and tree hashing repeats per owner.
- **Why it matters.** Adding an artifact kind, or changing sealing and selection semantics, repeats publication decisions. The potential value is one reusable store/seal/verify boundary (*Proposed*), independently of the accepted choice to retain host tools until explicit administrative cleanup. A content-addressed mechanism is a research question, not a selected architecture.
- **Patterns.** LOP-09, LOP-02, LOP-07.
- **Evidence.** Signatures across the files above; bodies of `server::seal`, `server::immutable`, `server::atomic_record`, `native::publish_pointer`, `product_reader::publish` with its `Evidence`, `prepared_xtask::publish` with its `Evidence`, and `generation::discover` and `from_resolution`.
- **Footprint and refresh.** The three previously unread publication bodies were read on 2026-11-02. Native publication seals, relocates and rechecks the tree; server publication writes a file-map receipt, seals, relocates, validates and switches its pointer; study modules publish with `NOREPLACE` and reconcile an existing identical receipt. Their transaction and collision semantics genuinely differ.
- **Preservation constraints.** Failed construction preserves selection; ordinary selection does not build; kind-specific provenance remains authoritative; source-aware cross-checkout copying preserves original build identity. The current no-automatic-collection contract replaces the removed `GenerationUse`/handoff claims. File-level primitives are [CBO-003](cross-cutting-mechanisms.md#cbo-003); this entry concerns the layer above them.
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** Some apparent duplication implements different durability and destination rules. A shared mechanism must reduce those decisions without broadening collection or homogenizing unlike identities. No selection-time cost was measured.
- **Status.** Current mechanism.
- **Research questions.** Which common artifact operations could a tree store supply while retaining each owner's publication/collision contract? Could a filesystem integrity mechanism remove repeated hashing? The ext-family observation is from 2026-10-30 and establishes neither present filesystem capabilities nor fs-verity enablement.

## CBO-055 — Arrow force-validation delivered by a Cargo argv and unit-graph adapter

- **Operation and consumers.** Every `cargo c`, `cargo t` and check or test recipe must build with validation for each Arrow-consuming root in the target and host graphs (ADR-0170), while pure closures stay pure. About 90 `justfile` lines name `cargo-pse-correctness`.
- **Current mechanism.** `crates/pse-host/src/correctness.rs` is about 1,100 non-test lines. It parses Cargo's global and subcommand grammar by hand (`mode_position`, `graph_arguments`, `metadata_arguments`, `check_controls`, `reject_reuse`); runs a preliminary `--unit-graph`; deserializes a local copy of the unstable schema (`Graph`, `Unit`) and a second partial `Metadata`; derives features, verifies leaf activation and re-executes. It impersonates `CARGO` to nextest (`ADAPTER`, `REAL_CARGO`) and to cargo-hack (`CARGO_HACK_CARGO_SRC`), needs `scripts/nextest-test-env` to restore real Cargo, and carries its own cfg and flag channel for taskdump (`DIAGNOSTIC_CFG`).
- **Why it matters.** Cargo never sees the intent, which is "feature X applies whenever the graph contains leaf Y". The wrapper is why arguments outside its allow-list refuse (`unsupported Cargo command envelope`), why every build pays a graph query, and why three tools need interposition. If a declared target-cfg dependency, a config flag or another Cargo-native selection could carry the same intent, the graph check would shrink to verification.
- **Patterns.** LOP-10, LOP-05, LOP-03.
- **Evidence and refresh.** The earlier adapter/argument reading was supplemented on 2026-11-02 by the complete `AdmissionContext::resolve_with` body and current correctness test declarations. Metadata is now reused within an invocation when its control arguments agree. The original graph still determines per-root additions; a changed selection produces a second graph and checks unchanged roots and actual Arrow leaf activation. All-feature diagnostic isolation can require another graph. This is narrower than the old premise that every stage repeats all preparation.
- **Footprint.** `discovery/capture::execute_build`, `selection` and `generation::from_resolution` consume the resulting `Resolution` and would still need compiler units whatever delivers the feature. Repeated resolution across one test run is [CBO-049](developer-tooling.md#cbo-049).
- **Preservation constraints.** Actual leaf activation is verified and unknown coverage refuses; host and build units are covered; pure closures get no unrelated roots; one feature set per dependency (ADR-0122); compiler JSON is unchanged; compile sharing between check and test.
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** This is the largest uncertainty on the page. A cfg-activated feature changes rustflags and so fingerprints, and host-context rustflags need care. Applying it to all roots conflicts with "pure closures stay pure", and workspace feature unification makes a plain always-on member wrong because product builds must not validate. The refusal may be valued as strictness, and the unit-graph capture also serves selection and freshness, so it may remain regardless. ADR-0170 and ADR-0175 are constraints to note, not limits.
- **Status.** Current mechanism.
- **Research questions.** On the pinned nightly, can dependency features be selected through config or `-Z` flags, or can `[target.'cfg(..)'.dependencies]` plus `--config` express this for host units too?

## CBO-056 — Invocation ledger has no retention, and queries scan every record

- **Operation and consumers.** `history`, `status`, `latest`, prefix selection, and the captures that accumulate under each invocation.
- **Current mechanism.** `InvocationStore::history` reads every directory and validates its `meta.json`, returning healthy records and structured independent source failures. `latest` refuses incomplete history; exact IDs and literal prefixes avoid opening unrelated metadata. Invocation-evidence retention remains distinct from rebuildable query pages and explicit probe removal. No general ledger-prune route was found in the inspected invocation/control owners.
- **Historical host observation.** On 2026-10-30 the checkout ledger held eight directories using 4.3 GiB; the retired `test_resources` family used about 2.2 GiB with 3,535 invocation entries. These were aggregate `du`/directory observations, not itemized attribution, and were not refreshed on 2026-11-02. They establish no current storage pressure or deletion authority.
- **Why it matters.** Per-invocation size is high, so an active checkout accumulates quickly. Disk space is maintainer-managed and no low-space guard is proposed; the gap is that the only tool offered is deleting state by hand, which must respect custody evidence for running work. A declared retention operation with liveness awareness would serve it. Query cost is small today, so an embedded index is a secondary question.
- **Patterns.** LOP-12, LOP-07.
- **Evidence and refresh.** Current `InvocationStore::{history,resolve}` bodies and `history_keeps_all_healthy_rows_and_structured_independent_source_failures` were inspected on 2026-11-02. The old `managed/recovery`, `local_claim` and generation-use owners were deleted and are not current retention prerequisites. Current original-outcome, custody and settled-work requirements must supply any future eligibility proof.
- **Footprint.** The invocation store only. Immutable generations have an explicit no-automatic-collection contract ([CBO-054](#cbo-054)); they are not evidence for a missing invocation retention policy. Historical orphaned state is the same class of residue as [CBO-065](developer-tooling.md#cbo-065).
- **Preservation constraints.** Evidence is write-once and the record owner stays authoritative; readers grant no control; unreadable sources stay visible as failures; a running or unsettled invocation is never removed; the store cannot depend on the SurrealDB service.
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** The per-file ledger is simple, durable and greppable, and an index adds a second truth. `latest` refusing on corruption is a stated contract. The earlier ext4 observation is not a current filesystem-capability check and does not settle whether captures could share storage. Retention itself may need no library.
- **Status.** Retained, narrowed. [Plan59 A04](../../plans/59a-cli-foundation.md#a04) owns responsibility-specific retention; its completion does not imply a general invocation garbage collector. The remaining opportunity is a concrete ledger maintenance/query operation consistent with original-evidence custody, whose need and cost remain *Proposed*.
- **Research questions.** Is a small embedded store worth it for history queries, and which retention rules (age, count, size) can be expressed from the evidence the host already writes?

## CBO-057 — Native provider construction is a hand-maintained build system

- **Operation and consumers.** `native prepare` builds or snapshots compiler and KLU, solver, isolation, Uno, PETSc and HiGHS prefixes; solver and scientific work consume them.
- **Current mechanism.** Four owners share the recipe. `docker/solvers/build.sh` (561 lines) with the Dockerfile and `checksums.sha256` builds oneMKL, METIS, MUMPS, SPRAL, ASL, Ipopt and SCIP. IBEX, Uno and PETSc archives and patches are pins under `[workspace.metadata.pse.{ibex,uno,petsc}]`. `native/providers.rs` (about 1,200 non-test lines) carries per-kind logic: `prepare_source` (about 230 lines of one CMake path, one PETSc configure path and a few provider branches), `prepare_solver` (pipes `docker run … tar` into `tar -xf`), `source_archive` (curl plus digest), `docker_runtime` (parses the Dockerfile for its pins). `docker/solvers/conda/env.yml` is a further partial recipe, and the solver README still speaks of CI jobs and devcontainer stages.
- **Why it matters.** A solver bump touches shell, Cargo metadata, host code and receipts, and each provider has its own freshness route. Having read `prepare_source` through, the burden is smaller than "a build system" suggests: what remains hand-owned is the provider relationships (BLAS paths into Uno and PETSc, HiGHS into Uno), relocation and the Docker extraction. The more useful outcome may be unifying the four owners and retiring stale CI and Windows premises rather than replacing the shell.
- **Patterns.** LOP-01, LOP-08, LOP-09.
- **Evidence.** Bodies of `prepare_source`, `prepare_external`, the `prepare_solver` extraction, `source_archive`, `pin`, `run_at`; the `build.sh` body (pins, checksum gate, verified-download shim, per-library steps); `conda/env.yml`; provider test names.
- **Footprint.** Not read: `verify_petsc`, `relocate_metadata`, `high_provider`, `native.rs` `compiler`. Vendored patches were not classified. Whether the Docker path is the actual origin of the local prefix was not established.
- **Preservation constraints.** Selection is source-free and launch never constructs; a failed build preserves selection; patched sources stay exact; one LP64 MKL with fixed threading and one OpenMP; no `target-cpu=native`; the compiler-cache bridge; the external-prefix option (`prepare_external`); `build-info` identity entering solve provenance.
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** `build.sh` is careful and specific: a universal checksum gate, a shim that serves only verified bytes to upstream scripts, flags chosen for one BLAS. Patched MKL and SPRAL combinations may stay custom under any package manager, and a Python-based concretizer would add a toolchain to a project retiring Python from product paths. Nix gives stronger hermeticity at higher migration cost. Native solver internals belong to [native-solver-integration](native-solver-integration.md); only the host side was assessed.
- **Status.** Current mechanism. Weak.
- **2026-11-02 reassessment.** Retained as weak after checking the current provider owner, exact-pin declarations and tests for failed-build selection, external-prefix snapshots, Uno/HiGHS and PETSc configuration. No provider construction was run. The code already exposes explicit external-prefix preparation, so research must compare the complete variant/relocation/provenance operation rather than merely replace shell syntax. Earlier full `prepare_source`/`prepare_external` reading remains dated 2026-10-30.
- **Research questions.** Could Spack environments, Pixi or Nix produce the exact variants relocatably and be consumed through the existing external-prefix seam?

## CBO-058 — Tool provisioning has several independent declarations

- **Operation and consumers.** Installing the developer's external tools, for the maintainer and agents through `just` recipes and `scripts/bootstrap.sh`.
- **Current mechanism.** `.config/diagnostic-tools.toml` with `diagnostic_tools.rs` provides hash-pinned HTTPS with curl, tar and zip stage limits. A `[cargo-tools]` table, with some versions in the TOML, sits beside the `CARGO_TOOLS` name array in `scripts/bootstrap.sh`, installed through `cargo binstall`. `cargo install cargo-binstall` and `cargo binstall ast-grep` are unpinned, actionlint comes from a `curl … main/… | bash` `latest`, shellcheck has apt notes, and the uv `quality` group is separate. Curl, tar and git also run as subprocesses in `native/providers::source_archive` and `server::acquire`, while `diagnostic_tools` uses the `tar` crate with limits and `archive_names` parses `tar -tvzf` text.
- **Why it matters.** A tool is declared differently depending on its packaging. The hash-pinned path is careful and the bootstrap path is not, so "an environment changes only on purpose" holds for some tools only. A version manager with a lockfile might own most of this. Sources in the subprocess cases are hash-pinned first, so that part is low consequence.
- **Patterns.** LOP-09, LOP-01.
- **Evidence.** `scripts/bootstrap.sh`, `.config/diagnostic-tools.toml`, `diagnostic_tools::install`, `cargo_tool_specs`, `scripts/setup-env`.
- **Footprint.** Inspected; `diagnostic_tools` stage bodies by signature.
- **Preservation constraints.** Passive verification never downloads; per-binary SHA-256 pinning and provenance; bounded extraction; the prepared `sccache` environment contract; `cargo-nextest` is protocol-sensitive to the extractor ([CBO-049](developer-tooling.md#cbo-049)).
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** Diagnostic tools include large contrib binaries and need explicit limits. A manager's trust model may be weaker than the current per-binary hash. Local-only use lowers the urgency.
- **Status.** Current mechanism.
- **2026-11-02 reassessment.** Retained after rereading `scripts/bootstrap.sh` and `.config/diagnostic-tools.toml`. New pinned diagnostic tools coexist with a name array whose undeclared Cargo tools receive no exact version. Actionlint still uses the upstream main-branch installer and `latest`; ast-grep and bootstrap's cargo-binstall are unpinned. This is an installation-declaration question, not a claim that the committed Rust/Python dependency lockfiles float. Passive verification and bounded extraction remain counterevidence against indiscriminate replacement.
- **Research questions.** Do `mise` or `aqua` lockfiles pin per-platform checksums for GitHub releases and Cargo tools, and can they express the existing size and extraction limits?

## CBO-059 — System facilities used through parallel spellings

- **Operation and consumers.** Developer tools and a little runtime that need Git, Cargo, systemd, download, archive and signal facilities.
- **Current mechanism.** Distinct sites, each beside a library already in the graph:
  - **Git.** About 10 production `Command::new("git")` sites across `xtask/src/documentation/{register,adr}.rs`, `development/repository_tools.rs`, `codegen.rs` (`untracked_check`) and `pse-buildinfo::git_context`, while `pse-host` uses `gix` for status, affected-diff and source reads. `adr::object_state` makes several process round trips per file across all ADRs, and `git_context` polls `try_wait` every 10 ms to honour cancellation.
  - **Cargo metadata.** `xtask/src/codegen/grpc_wire.rs::append` shells to `cargo metadata` and walks `serde_json::Value` although xtask already uses `cargo_metadata` ([CBO-051](developer-tooling.md#cbo-051)).
  - **systemd.** `pse-operations/src/canonical_files.rs` runs `systemctl --user show` and parses text for `InvocationID` and `ControlGroup`, while the `zbus_systemd` proxies live only in `pse-host/src/manager.rs`. This is the most consequential site because it sits in a managed-file ownership check.
  - **Download, archive, signal.** `diagnostic_tools::install` shells to curl; `xtask/src/codegen/ipopt.rs` pipes to `tar`; `pse-runtime/src/bin/support/signals.rs` uses `kill` although rustix `process` is enabled.
- **Why it matters.** PATH-dependent effects, text parsing and differing failure typing. Each shell-out is small and behaves; the value is uniformity, and mostly the systemd case.
- **Patterns.** LOP-03, LOP-09, LOP-08.
- **Evidence.** All named sites read; `rg 'Command::new("<tool>")'` locates them (dynamically named commands are not covered).
- **Footprint.** The `paths.rs` git commands are test fixtures and were excluded.
- **Preservation constraints.** The host stays cold and small, so adding an HTTP and TLS stack there is a real cost; curl's `env_clear`, https-only, size and time limits; `pse-operations` cannot depend on `pse-host`, so a systemd query needs a lower owner; shallow-repository `Unknown`, `HEAD` meaning and `git show origin/main` ordering semantics.
- **Counterevidence and uncertainty.** `pse-buildinfo` reaches product code, so gix there has compile weight. Log with `--diff-filter=A` needs a tree-diff walk in gix, which may be wordier. Subprocess Git is complete, simple and cold-start friendly. `signals.rs` is in a bin support path and may be irrelevant.
- **Status.** Current mechanism.
- **2026-11-02 reassessment.** Retained after checking current literal subprocess consumers: `canonical_files.rs` still shells to `systemctl` for ownership facts; `pse-buildinfo/src/identity.rs`, documentation/codegen and repository tools still use Git; runtime signal support still invokes `kill`. The new gix-backed source/history tools do not migrate those consumers automatically. Only named literal commands were searched; dynamic dispatch and all error branches were not traced. The systemd custody query remains the strongest research target.
- **Research questions.** Can `gix` answer the ADR history and immutability queries with typed results? Which lower crate could own the systemd query for `pse-operations`?

## Examined and not retained

- **`affected` hand-builds reach from Cargo metadata.** `Workspace::read` builds a reverse map from `dependency.path` or name and version and widens to `UnknownDependency` for optional or target-conditional declarations. It is about 150 lines, preview-only and honest about uncertainty; widening frequency is unknown and the generator-reach logic stays regardless. A graph library (`guppy`, `determinator`) is a lead of modest value.
- **Replacing systemd transient-unit control with a process-supervisor crate.** The manager model (scopes, `AddRef`, `BindsTo`, job signals, kernel-verified population) is the mechanism itself.
- **Replacing `managed/streams` or `RecordFramer`, or journald as the raw log store.** Per-chunk write deadlines with a confirmed prefix, `io_unresolved` custody, byte-accurate offsets and a synchronous observer are contract, and no library read offered them.
- **Replacing admission's flock-and-JSON journal with an embedded database.** The journal is small, revisioned and bound to the manager's identity, and needs one writer at a time.
- **Reflink test-binary captures.** `stat -f` reports an ext-family filesystem where plain FICLONE is unavailable, and hardlinks do not give the immutability the capture wants.
- **Landlock or systemd sandboxing around agents.** Allow-list shaped, and the repository states the guard is not a sandbox; no current need shown.
- **`HostError::Ownership(String)` with about 1,300 `invalid(...)` sites.** A typing question no library changes.
