## 2. Decomposition, ownership and dependencies

## 3. Contracts, authority and constraints

Incumbent opt-ins cannot waive original feasibility, model domains, missing required closure or downstream coverage. A terminal endpoint records pre-termination mode and inputs without a reset. Historical absence cannot be inferred into current qualification. Each schema transition admits exact source, target and checksum with atomic history and readiness.

## 7. Findings

No proposed MUST gap remains in the bounded design. Implementation must verify original-sense MIP bounds, terminal quadrature domains, permit lifetime through panic cleanup and durable historical absence. These are execution obligations owned by Plan 25e, not established implementation facts.

## 8. Library fit and ownership cost

Retain established native solvers, library matching, Symbolica/faer mathematics and current supervisors. PostgreSQL supplies transaction and locking semantics; a pinned migration library is preferred where compatible. Generated transport changes require complete Rust/Python migration and owner-issued identity versions.

## 9. Alternatives and tradeoffs

Combined fixture tags, reconstructed defaults and local success booleans are simpler locally but duplicate authority. Appending roots to requested grids conceals observation semantics. Destructive reset loses history. Explicit admitted products and transitions cost migration work while making evidence and refusal attributable.
